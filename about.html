How the Open MSME catalog works

An open catalog — and a build roadmap — of software for small businesses in India and the Philippines.

What this is

Open MSME is a catalog of 500 software tools for micro, small and medium enterprises across 16 sectors in two markets: India and the Philippines. Every tool is free and open-source. This site is the index; each tool, as it is built, gets its own open-source repository and its own explainer site, linked from its card here.

Who it is for

Resource-constrained businesses that can't afford per-seat SaaS, and the developers, students and local technologists who want to build useful things for them. The problems are deliberately specific to these two markets — Indian GST and Udyam rules, Philippine BIR and local-permit rules — because that specificity is what makes a tool actually usable instead of a generic template.

How ideas earn their place

The catalog started as a much larger pool and was cut down hard. Anything vague, generic, not genuinely useful to a small business, or not realistically buildable was dropped, and near-duplicates were merged so that each entry is a distinct product. What remains is 500 ideas, each tied to a real job and, where relevant, a named regulation.

What the first wave means

Software has a maintenance tail, so the whole catalog is not built at once. A first wave of 15 tools is built ahead of the rest, chosen to cover many sectors and to ship quickly. An idea marked Idea is on the roadmap; one marked First wave is being built now; one marked Live is released and free to use today, with its source public. Nothing here is claimed as finished until its repository is public.

Open-source & licensing

Every tool is released under a permissive open-source licence (MIT), so a business can run it for free and a developer can fork, adapt or localise it. The goal is practical innovation that stays in the hands of the businesses it serves.

The data

The full catalog is published as machine-readable JSON at data/catalog.json, so anyone can analyse it, build on it, or propose additions through the source repository.
