MSME-006

Textile Unit-Costing Calculator

Live

Problem. Small units price by gut, forgetting wastage, job-work and overhead, so they either sell below cost or lose orders by overpricing.

Who it helps. Handloom weavers, powerloom units, small garment makers, dye houses.

What it does. Enter yarn cost and count, warp/weft consumption, wastage %, job-work (dye/print/embroidery), labour and overhead to get true per-meter/per-piece cost, break-even, margin and suggested price bands.

Regulation. GST input costing, Udyam/BMBE for formal invoicing

MSME-007

Yarn Inventory by Count, Lot & Shade

First wave

Problem. Yarn is the biggest cost and a stockout halts every loom, yet units track it as loose kilos with no count/lot/shade granularity, so they over-buy or run dry.

Who it helps. Weaving sheds, garment units, dye houses, cooperatives.

What it does. Track yarn by count (e.g. 2/60s), colour/dye-lot and supplier; record issue/receipt, compute consumption against production, and alert on reorder points.

Regulation. GST HSN on yarn, e-way bill on inward, Udyam

MSME-607

AQL Finished-Garment Inspection Logger

Idea

Problem. Final inspection uses ANSI/ASQ Z1.4 AQL tables that staff read off dog-eared printouts; they pull the wrong sample size or accept/reject number, and a buyer audit that catches wrong sampling can reject a whole shipment.

Who it helps. Garment QC inspectors, buying-house QA and factory final-inspection desks.

What it does. Enter lot size, inspection level and AQL (e.g. 2.5 major / 4.0 minor); the app returns the exact sample size and accept/reject numbers, then logs defects by critical/major/minor with photos and renders a pass/fail inspection report the buyer will accept.

Regulation. ANSI/ASQ Z1.4; ISO 2859-1 (AQL acceptance sampling)

MSME-608

Apparel Export Time & Action (T&A) Critical-Path Calendar

Idea

Problem. Each export order is a chain of deadlines - fabric in-house, trims in, cutting, sewing, finishing, final inspection, ex-factory, on-board - and one silent slip cascades into an airfreight bill or a cancelled order.

Who it helps. Garment export merchandisers and production planners juggling many buyer POs.

What it does. From the ship date and PO, auto-build a backward critical-path T&A plan with standard lead times per stage, flag amber/red as stages slip, and show a portfolio view of every order's health so planners chase the one task that actually threatens delivery.

MSME-075

Idea

MSME-611

Textile ZLD & CETP Effluent Compliance Log

Idea

Problem. Pollution boards and ZLD mandates require daily effluent flow, pH and discharge logs, flow-meter readings and CETP inlet records; a missing log or a lapsed Consent to Operate can get a dye house sealed overnight.

Who it helps. Dyeing and processing houses in Tirupur, Pali, Panipat, Surat and other CETP-member clusters.

What it does. Log daily effluent flow, pH, TDS and CETP/ZLD meter readings against consented limits, auto-flag excursions, and track Consent-to-Operate and Hazardous-Waste authorisation renewal dates with escalating reminders; export the register in the format the SPCB/CETP inspector expects.

Regulation. SPCB Consent to Operate (Water Act 1974); ZLD norms; Hazardous Waste authorisation

Idea

MSME-613

Embroidery Stitch-Count Pricing & Zari Karigar Ledger

Idea

Problem. Embroidery is priced by stitch count, head-hours or per motif, and precious zari and stones are issued to karigars by weight; without a system, jobs are under-priced and material (including gold/silver zari) goes unaccounted.

Who it helps. Machine (Schiffli/multi-head) and hand (aari, zardozi) embroidery units and job-workers.

MSME-614

Fabric Bolt (Thaan) Barcode & Remnant-Length Tracker

Idea

Problem. Cloth is sold by the metre off bolts (thaan); staff cannot tell how many metres remain on a roll without unrolling it, so they over-promise, lose short remnants and cannot value stock accurately.

Who it helps. Fabric wholesalers, retailers and warehouse stores selling cloth by the metre.

What it does. Give each bolt a barcode tagging design, shade, width and starting length; every cut scans the bolt and deducts metres, so remaining length, rack location and value are always known, with alerts on low remnants and fast-moving designs for reorder.

Idea
